## DETAILED FINDINGS


### A. Design & First Impression

- The site uses Twenty Twenty, the default WordPress theme from 2019. It looks generic and dated next to cisternofhopefoundation.org and dckabeten.org.
- The logo is shown very large (about 330 x 273 pixels) in the header, pushing content down, especially on phones.
- Several pages show a stray Categories label above the page title, which is a leftover from the theme.
- The footer only says (c) 2026 Sylvia's Dream, Powered by WordPress.com. There is no contact, social media, newsletter or policy information there.
- Many images are AI-generated and still carry their original file names (for example ChatGPT Image Apr 19, 2026). Real photos of Sylvia and of the work in Kenya build far more trust.

### B. Navigation & Site Structure

- The main menu has 28 links, including every individual book. Visitors are overwhelmed and cannot see the key paths: books, about, ministry, give, contact.
- The Home link in the menu does not go to the home page. It goes to /sylvias-dream/, which is actually a From Fear to Freedom book page.
- There are three home pages (the real home page, /home/ and /new-home/), all public.
- Free Downloads appears twice in the menu, and Fast Forward to Success is labelled Fast Foreword.
- Duplicate pages compete with each other: From Fear to Freedom (3 pages), The Hidden Key to Healing and Breakthrough (3), From Chance to Choice (2), All Shall Be Well (2), How to Find the Real You (2), and My Story / My Autobiography covering the same ground.
- Some content exists both as a page and as a blog post (for example You Are Deeply Loved by God, and To Ensure You End Up Going to Heaven).

### C. Home Page

- The home page is short (about 115 words) and focuses on a single book. It does not quickly explain who Sylvia is, which book to start with, or how to support Heaven on Earth.
- There is no clear newsletter or free ebook sign-up on the home page, although a free ebook offer exists on another page.
- The browser tab title is about 150 characters long with extra spaces, and the search description is taken automatically from the first lines of text (Explore the Books Not sure where to begin?...).

### D. Book Shop & Buying Experience

- There is no single shop or cart. Books are sold through about 10 separate Stripe payment links, plus Amazon, Kobo, Barnes & Noble, Spotify, 24symbols and Lulu links (some through bit.ly short links).
- The Shop for Books page has two main headings, mixed button styles (buy now, buy paperback, buy and download now) and dotted divider lines instead of a clean layout.
- Prices are only in AUD and are shown inconsistently from book to book.
- Some retail links are very long Amazon search links with tracking codes; these can break and look untidy. Each book should have one clean link per store.
- The Manifesting Miracles Now page still says Coming Soon.

### E. Security of Paid Ebooks (High Priority)

- After payment, Stripe sends buyers to a thank-you page with a download link. These 16 thank-you and download pages are public and listed in the site's sitemap, which tells Google about them.
- The ebook files themselves sit in the public media library. We confirmed that paid files, including From Fear to Freedom and Guard Your Heart, open directly without any payment.
- Some downloads are Word (.docx) manuscript files, such as Kindle preparation files, rather than finished ebooks. These can be copied and edited by anyone.
- Recommended fix: move to a shop that issues private, expiring download links per buyer, remove these pages from search, and replace the public files.

### F. Heaven on Earth & Dream Partner Giving

- The page clearly explains the mission, the monthly levels and that about 90% of donations go to programmes. This is a good base.
- It describes Heaven on Earth as a registered charity but does not show the registration details. Donors look for this before they give.
- Giving is split across several Stripe links and a GoFundMe page. One clear Give page with a progress bar toward 100 Dream Partners would convert better.
- 20 of the 21 images on this page have no description, and four YouTube videos are embedded on one page, which slows it down on mobile data.

### G. Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)

- No structured data (schema) is set up, so Google does not clearly understand Sylvia as an author, her books, or the charity.
- The image shown when the site is shared on Facebook or WhatsApp is a QR code, not a book or a photo of Sylvia.
- Eight blog posts share the same title, Grace for the Journey, so Google sees them as competing duplicates. Two post addresses contain emoji characters.
- The Free Resource for Mission Training page has the wrong main heading (Sylvia's Dream) even though it is over 3,000 words long.
- Duplicate pages and the public thank-you pages split search rankings and should be merged or hidden with redirects.

### H. Accessibility

- 117 of the 171 images we checked across the site have no alt text, so screen readers and Google cannot understand them.
- Several headings are written in all capitals and some titles use emoji, which screen readers read out awkwardly.
- Buttons use inconsistent wording and styles, which makes the next step less obvious for older readers in particular.

### I. Trust, Contact & Legal

- There is no Privacy Policy, Terms of Sale or Refund Policy, although the site sells ebooks, collects emails and takes donations. These are expected under the Australian Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Consumer Law, and by Stripe and PayPal.
- The Contact page shows a home street address publicly. We recommend showing only an email and a contact form, or a postal box.
- The contact email is a personal Gmail address. A branded address (for example hello@sylviasdream.com) looks more professional.
- Social media links (Instagram, Patreon, YouTube) are scattered across pages instead of being shown consistently in the header or footer.

## RECOMMENDED APPROACH

- We recommend improving the existing site rather than starting again. Your domain, WordPress.com plan, blog posts, subscribers and Stripe account all stay as they are. The work is done on a private copy of the new design and switched on once you approve it, so the live site keeps selling throughout.
- Phase 1, Protect (days 1-2). Hide the thank-you pages from search, secure the ebook files and agree the content plan and book list.
- Phase 2, Design (days 2-5). Modern theme in the warm gold and cream style, large readable text, homepage and page designs for your approval.
- Phase 3, Restructure (days 3-7). New menu, one home page, merged book pages, a single About Sylvia page and redirects from all old addresses.
- Phase 4, Shop, Giving & Sign-up (days 5-9). One shop with cart and secure downloads for your full catalogue, Dream Partner giving and email sign-up.
- Phase 5, SEO, Policies & Testing (days 8-11). Titles, alt text, schema, policy pages, analytics, and test purchases and gifts.
- Phase 6, Launch & Support (days 10-12, then 3 months). Your final approvals, training, launch, and 3 months of free support.

## RISKS & HOW WE MANAGE THEM

- WordPress.com plan limits. Some shop features need a Business-level plan. We confirm this on day 1; any upgrade is your decision and is not part of the quotation.
- Disruption to sales. All changes are prepared and tested before switching on, so existing buyers and links keep working.
- Broken old links. Every merged or retired page is redirected, so links shared on social media, in books and in emails still work.
- Existing buyers. People who already bought ebooks will be offered a new secure link if old download pages are removed.
- Delays. Late content or feedback extends the timeline but not the price.
- Time zones. Kenya and Australia are 7.5 to 8 hours apart during Australian daylight saving, so updates are shared by WhatsApp, email and short scheduled calls.
